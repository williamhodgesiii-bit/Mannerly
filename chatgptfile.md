# Mannerly — How the App Works (User's Perspective)

*A plain-language summary you can hand to ChatGPT (or anyone) to explain what
Mannerly is and what using it feels like.*

---

## What Mannerly is

Mannerly is **"Duolingo for manners, etiquette, and cultural fluency."** It teaches
everyday social skills — greetings, listening, table manners, digital etiquette, and
country-specific customs — through short, interactive lessons instead of long articles
or passive videos. The promise is **confidence in every culture**: *"I know what to do."*

It runs as one app on the web, the Apple App Store, and Google Play. It's built for
kids, teens, adults, parents, teachers, travelers, and professionals, and it adapts to
each of them.

The whole experience feels like a game, not a textbook. Here's how it works, end to end.

---

## 1. You tell it who you are (onboarding)

Onboarding is a friendly, one-question-at-a-time conversation. It opens with
**"How will you use Mannerly?"** and branches four ways:

- **For myself** — You pick your age band (Kids 5–7 · Tweens 8–12 · Teens 13–17 ·
  Adults 18+), where you live (your **home region**, which stays free forever), your
  language, and what you want to get better at (work, travel, dining, confidence,
  friendships, and more). You try a quick interactive starter scenario, choose whether
  you'd like a **Daily Manner** reminder, set any accessibility preferences (larger text,
  reduced motion, higher contrast), and you're in.
- **For my family** — One grown-up sets up the household, then adds a profile for each
  child (name, age, avatar — no email or login for kids). Each child learns on their own
  progress, and a **"Who's learning?"** switcher swaps between them.
- **I'm a teacher** — You spin up a class and get a shareable **join code**
  (like `MANNER-3207`).
- **I'm a student** — You type your class code, pick an avatar, and jump straight into a
  lesson.

Lessons adapt by age — younger kids get simple, concrete actions with little reading;
teens get social media, group chats, parties, and reputation; adults get professional,
travel, and hosting situations.

---

## 2. You land on your path (Home)

Home is a **winding trail of lesson "nodes"** — like a game map — with a glowing
**START** button on the next lesson waiting for you. A banner at the top shows the
"world" you're currently in (the free Global Core, or a country), how far you've come,
and your current level. In the corner you always see two things:

- 🔥 **Streak** — how many days in a row you've practiced.
- ⚡ **Charge** — your energy for lessons (explained below).

---

## 3. You play a lesson (the heart of it)

Each lesson is fast — about a minute or two — and follows the same satisfying loop:

1. **See the situation.** An animated stage sets the scene — a home, a restaurant, a
   train, a temple — with friendly characters.
2. **Choose what you'd do.** You pick from a few options (e.g. keep your shoes on, take
   them off, or ask the host).
3. **See what happens.** The character reacts immediately, with a little buzz of haptic
   feedback. Pick the "wrong" one? There's **no penalty** — you just try again. (Mannerly
   rewards learning; it never punishes mistakes.)
4. **Learn _why_.** You get the reason it matters, how formal the setting is, how strong
   the custom is, **what to do when you're unsure**, the exceptions, and the real sources
   the lesson is drawn from. It teaches *judgment*, not rigid rules — and never presents a
   custom as universal when it isn't.
5. **Get a real-world challenge.** A small mission to actually try in life — "Introduce
   yourself to someone new today," "Put your phone away during dinner."
6. **Get rewarded.** Confetti, the **XP** you earned, and your **streak** ticking up.

---

## 4. You keep it bite-sized (charge / energy)

Each lesson spends one **bolt** of charge. You get five bolts, and one refills every
30 minutes. It's a gentle nudge to learn a little and come back later — the same rhythm
that keeps sessions short and habit-forming. **Manners+**, **Family**, and **School**
plans make charge unlimited.

---

## 5. You explore the world (Explore)

The **Explore** tab is a shelf of "worlds," grouped by region. It includes the free
**Global Manners Core** plus **eight country packs**:

> 🇯🇵 Japan · 🇰🇷 South Korea · 🇦🇪 UAE · 🇮🇳 India · 🇲🇽 Mexico · 🇧🇷 Brazil ·
> 🇺🇸 USA · 🇬🇧 UK

Tap any world to make it your active path. Free packs are open to everyone; the rest
unlock with a subscription.

---

## 6. You collect and level up

- **Manners Passport** — You earn a **stamp for every country you learn**, plus
  achievements like First Steps, a 3-day streak, Week Strong, Globetrotter, and
  World Citizen.
- **Levels** — Your XP carries you up a ladder of mastery:
  **Beginner → Comfortable → Confident → Ambassador.**

---

## 7. It's yours everywhere (the Me tab & accounts)

The **Me** tab is your identity — your avatar, your level, your stats (streak, total XP,
lessons completed, passport stamps), your learning goals, and your settings (age group,
language, home region, account).

You can sign in, and your profile and progress follow you to any device. The business
model is **freemium, not ads**:

- **Free forever** — the Global Manners Core, your home region, and a taste of every
  country pack. Basic manners are never locked away.
- **Manners+** — unlocks every country, travel mode, advanced scenarios, and unlimited
  practice.
- **Family plan** — several profiles under one household, so parents and kids learn
  together.
- **Travel Packs** — a one-time purchase for a single country, aimed at travelers who
  just need one trip's worth of etiquette.
- **Schools** — classroom licensing with teacher assignment and progress tools.

---

## What makes it different

- **It teaches judgment, not memorization.** Every cultural lesson answers three
  questions: *what's expected, why, and what to do when you're unsure* — with sources, a
  reviewer, and a review date.
- **It rewards real life, not just tapping.** The real-world challenge at the end of each
  lesson pushes the learning off the screen and into actual behavior.
- **It's respectful and accurate.** Content is country- and situation-specific (never
  "Asian manners" or other broad, stereotype-prone labels) and is written to be updated
  as customs change.
